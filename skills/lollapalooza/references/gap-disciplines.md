# Mental-model gap disciplines

Five mental models from math/probability and biology/evolution that have no dedicated agent elsewhere in this plugin. Each entry gives the underlying mechanism and a trigger→action rule for applying it in the moment.

## 1. Compound interest and the time value of compounding

**Mechanism:** Small, consistent advantages compound geometrically over enough repetitions, not linearly. A modest edge repeated many times outgrows a larger one-time edge because each period's gain compounds on top of all prior gains, not just the original base.

**Trigger → action:** Favor decisions that compound — recurring advantages, repeatable plays — over one-shot wins of similar apparent size.

**Source:** Charles T. Munger, "A Lesson on Elementary, Worldly Wisdom As It Relates To Investment Management and Business" (USC Business School, 1994).

## 2. Permutations, combinations, and expected value

**Mechanism:** Evaluate a decision under uncertainty by its probability-weighted range of outcomes, not its single most-likely one. Counting the actual space of possible outcomes (permutations and combinations) and weighting each by its probability reveals a fuller picture than reasoning from the modal case alone.

**Trigger → action:** When a decision has a small chance of a very large downside, expected value alone understates the risk — check the tail, not just the average.

**Source:** Charles T. Munger, "A Lesson on Elementary, Worldly Wisdom As It Relates To Investment Management and Business" (USC Business School, 1994).

## 3. Base rates

**Mechanism:** The outside view: what generally happens to decisions or ventures of this shape, before adjusting for this case's specifics. Starting from the reference class's historical frequency anchors judgment before case-specific narrative has a chance to bias it.

**Trigger → action:** State the base rate explicitly before arguing why this case is different — most "this time is different" reasoning is wrong; people systematically underweight the outside view.

**Source:** Charles T. Munger, "A Lesson on Elementary, Worldly Wisdom As It Relates To Investment Management and Business" (USC Business School, 1994).

## 4. Natural selection and competitive advantage (biology)

**Mechanism:** An advantage only persists if it's genuinely hard for competitors to replicate, the same way a biological adaptation only persists under real selection pressure. An edge that any rival can copy without cost is a temporary lead, not a durable trait — selection pressure only preserves what resists imitation.

**Trigger → action:** Ask what stops a competitor from copying this advantage next quarter — if nothing does, it isn't a moat, it's a head start.

**Source:** Charles T. Munger, "A Lesson on Elementary, Worldly Wisdom As It Relates To Investment Management and Business" (USC Business School, 1994).

## 5. Ecological niches

**Mechanism:** Organisms, and businesses, that avoid direct competition by occupying an underused niche often outcompete those fighting head-on in a crowded one. Fitness is relative to the specific niche being contested, not absolute — a competitor that shifts to less-contested ground can win even without being stronger overall.

**Trigger → action:** Before competing directly on an incumbent's own terms, check whether an adjacent, less-contested niche serves the same underlying goal.

**Source:** Charles T. Munger, "A Lesson on Elementary, Worldly Wisdom As It Relates To Investment Management and Business" (USC Business School, 1994).

## Checklist

- Favor decisions that compound — recurring advantages, repeatable plays — over one-shot wins of similar apparent size.
- Check the tail risk, not just expected value, when a decision carries a small chance of a very large downside.
- State the base rate before arguing why this case is different.
- Ask what stops a competitor from copying an advantage next quarter — if nothing does, it isn't a moat.
- Consider an adjacent, less-contested niche before competing head-on on an incumbent's own terms.
- Treat any claimed edge as temporary until you've checked both how it compounds and how it survives against a base rate.
