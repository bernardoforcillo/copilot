# Jobs-to-be-done brief: <job>

> Date: <YYYY-MM-DD> · Author: <name> · Evidence: <n> conversations, <n> observed sessions

A one-page statement of the job before anyone proposes a solution. The discipline is Clayton
Christensen's: people don't buy products, they hire something to make progress in a circumstance.
Get the circumstance right and the solution space narrows on its own.

## The job story

**When** <situation — the trigger, with enough specificity to recognize it>,
**I want to** <motivation, stated as progress, not as a feature>,
**so I can** <expected outcome — how they'd know they succeeded>.

If the "want to" names a feature ("I want a dashboard"), it's a solution wearing a job's clothes.
Ask what they'd do with it.

## The circumstance

- **Trigger**: what happens immediately before? How often, and to how many?
- **Current solution**: what are they doing today — including spreadsheets, a colleague, or
  nothing. "Nothing" is a real competitor and usually the strongest one.
- **What it costs them today**: time, money, risk, or annoyance, with numbers where they exist.

## Forces

| Force | What we heard |
| --- | --- |
| **Push** — dissatisfaction with today | |
| **Pull** — attraction of a new way | |
| **Anxiety** — fear of the new thing | |
| **Habit** — attachment to the current way | |

Push and pull sell; anxiety and habit are why they don't switch, and they're the half most teams
never address. A feature that increases pull while ignoring anxiety moves no one.

## Success and failure criteria (theirs, not ours)

How the person judges whether they got the progress they hired us for — in their words. This is
what a metric should proxy, and where a proxy will eventually detach.

## Evidence

Who was spoken to, how many, how selected, and what was *observed* rather than reported. Mark
inferences as inferences. Continuous-discovery discipline (Teresa Torres): a weekly touch with
someone doing the job beats a quarterly research project.

## What would falsify this

The observation that would show the job isn't real or isn't ours to serve. Written now, while it's
still cheap to be wrong.
